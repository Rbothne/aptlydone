What changed on the tracker, newest first, written while you were away. This page is temporary. Times are US Eastern, 18 and 19 November 2026.

Published about 11:30 pm, 19 November filter by who lives nearby: median age, degrees, race and ethnicity

  • New filter: "Who lives nearby". It sits next to Areas and opens a small panel with a "from" and a "to" box for each of the figures shown under "About the area": median age, the share of adults with a degree, and the share of residents who are white, Black, Asian, Hispanic or other. Leave a box empty for no limit. The figures are for the building's census tract.
  • It works like the other filters: on the list, the map and the Followed tab, remembered in this browser, cleared by Reset, and carried in the settings export. A building with no figure for something you limit is hidden while the limit is set.
  • Compared as shown. A tract with a median age of 38.6 shows as "Median age 39", so "from 39" keeps it.
  • Filter only (my call, and the rule this page already had for race): none of these feeds a deal tag or Top picks. The race and ethnicity boxes follow the switch at the bottom of the page: switched off, they disappear and stop applying, and come back as you left them when it is switched on again.
  • Worth knowing if this page is ever opened to other people: the big rental sites leave out filters by race on purpose, because fair-housing law forbids steering renters by race. Choosing for yourself is your own business; offering the filter to the public is a different matter. The one switch above turns it off.

Published about 6:30 pm, 19 November Followed is the buildings list, narrowed to what you follow

  • One list, not two. The Followed tab used to have "Buildings you follow" and "Units you follow", and a followed unit sat alone on a bare card. Now it is the same list as Buildings, with the same cards, filters and order, narrowed to buildings you follow. Following a unit (the ☆) puts its whole building there, with every listing that matches your filters.
  • The filters apply there too. Change beds, price or anything else and the Followed tab changes with it, like the main list. The line under the filters counts followed buildings while you are on that tab.
  • What you followed never drops out (my call). A unit you followed shows first in its building even when the filters would hide it, and stays as last seen once it is no longer listed. A building you followed stays when nothing in it matches ("Nothing here matches your filters right now"), and a no-go ZIP does not hide it.
  • Gone: "Follow building" no longer remembers the size and price filters of the day you pressed it. Buildings you already follow are kept; they now show whatever matches today's filters.

Published about 1:15 pm, 19 November locations audited and corrected, the map and the list work together

  • Location audit: the ZIP codes were the weird part. I sent every building's street address to the Census Bureau's address service and compared the answer with what the page was showing. The ZIP a building publishes agreed with the Census Bureau every single time both were usable (444 of 444). The ZIP the page was actually showing, looked up from the map coordinates, was wrong for 246 of 564 buildings (AVA Ballston showed 22213; it is 22201). That lookup is now the last resort: the building's own ZIP comes first, then the Census Bureau's (used for 16 Bozzuto buildings that put their street number where the ZIP belongs, and 20 Cortland buildings that publish none). Two things to check: ZIP rent trends were attached to the wrong ZIP for those buildings and are now right, and any no-go ZIP codes you set may now hide different buildings than before.
  • Coordinates were mostly right; eight buildings were not. The Census Bureau found 525 of the 585 addresses. Published coordinates were within a tenth of a mile of the address for all but 56, and most of those are large properties. Eight sat more than a quarter mile from an address the Census Bureau matched exactly, and are now placed by their address: Gallery 64 (1.4 miles off: its site had it at H Street by North Capitol, it is on H Street SW), Bayshore Landing in Annapolis (1.2), Crest at Skyland Town Center (1.0), Brandywine Green (0.8), 2701 Connecticut Ave (0.65), 2629 39th St (0.33), Windsor at Fair Lakes (0.28) and Signature Reston Town Center (0.27). Their crime counts, nearby places, noise, walkability and area figures were all collected again for the new spot.
  • The rule from now on (checked every week): a building's own site is believed for its coordinates unless an exactly matched address is more than a quarter mile away; then the address wins. Where the Census Bureau had to guess (five cases, such as "301 West Broad St" matched to East Broad), the site is kept. Sixty addresses the Census Bureau could not find at all (many look like new streets), so those rest on the site's coordinates alone. The quarter mile is my call.
  • MAA's buildings said "DC Metro" where the city belongs. They now show the city of their address (Alexandria, Arlington, Centreville, Rockville, McLean as "Mc Lean", the post office's spelling).
  • Still odd, not fixed: neighbourhood names come from OpenStreetMap and some are not neighbourhoods: "Ward 3" (6 buildings), "North Capitol Street" (3), a few Alexandria school-district names, and 31 DC buildings that just say "Washington". DC publishes official neighbourhood names; using those is the fix if you want it.
  • Map: "Go to it in the list" no longer filters everything else away. It switches to the list with your filters as they were, scrolls to that building and outlines it, so you can mark its ZIP no-go and carry on.
  • Map and list keep their places. Switching tabs returns you to where you were in each: the list at the same scroll position, the map at the same view with its pop-up still open. Each building in the list has an "On the map" button that opens the map on it.
  • Map pop-up. It now shows the building's best deal (the matching unit furthest under similar units, never one marked "Check price") and how that unit compares with its own building and with nearby buildings, then the block's walkability, the area's median age, and race and ethnicity (hidden if you have switched that off at the bottom of the page).
  • DC violations: your link has a download. The dashboard gives 5,711 violations, but only since 1 October (the city's current budget year). 27 of the 212 tracked DC buildings have any; most have none; a few have many (two at 45, one at 24, one at 17). Virginia: I looked for Fairfax, Arlington and Alexandria and found no case-by-case data, only summaries (the one lead was Arlington's list of stop-work and unsafe-building notices, which I have not opened).
  • Is it worth building? Yes, as a red flag, not as a score. In both DC and Montgomery most buildings have little or nothing and a handful have a lot, which is exactly the handful you would want warned about (rodents, insects, no heat). It would show a warning on roughly one building in ten and say nothing about the rest. Not built yet: say the word.
  • Review tagging before you see the page: not allowed by Google. Google's terms forbid fetching reviews ahead of time or storing them, and forbid showing its place content alongside a non-Google map, which this page has. So neither the overnight scan nor a simple in-page look-up fits their rules. I have not found another source of review text that allows it.

Published about 1 pm, 19 November price drops, noise, construction, balconies and own doors, crime against the typical, a step-by-step setup

  • Price drop tag and sort. A unit is tagged "Price drop" when its rent is at least 2% below the highest it stood at in the last 30 days and its latest move was down. It is in the Tags drop-down under Deals, and a unit can carry it next to a steal tag. "Biggest price drop" is a new sort. The tracker has only two days of history, so today's drops are all from the last day or two (about 260 units). The 2% floor and the 30 days are my calls: pricing software nudges rents by a few dollars daily, and without a floor everything would be tagged.
  • Violent crime is now measured against the typical building. The slider steps through a quarter, half, three quarters, the same as, 1.5, 2 and 3 times the typical building's count, and shows the real number beside each ("half the typical or less (9 or fewer)"). Typical is the middle building of the 517 tracked buildings that have counts: today 19 violent crimes within half a mile in a year. Each building's crime line says how it compares ("24 violent (1.3× the typical building's 19)"). An old fixed limit you had set is dropped; set it again on the new slider.
  • Aircraft, road and rail noise. From the US Department of Transportation's noise map (2020): sound averaged over 24 hours, in bands. 34 buildings sit in 50 decibels or more of aircraft noise (mostly along the river approach to Reagan National), 2 of them in 55 or more; 20 are in 60 or more of road noise and 11 of rail. Those show a note on the building, and the tag "No aircraft noise" hides the 34. The Department says the map shows patterns and is not exact for one address, so read a note as "look into this". Which levels earn a note is my call.
  • Construction, from building permits rather than reviews. You asked to scan reviews for construction. None of the pages the tracker reads carries review text, only a star rating, so there is nothing to scan yet (see "Needs you" below). Permits answer the same worry more directly, and DC publishes them: of the 212 DC buildings, 41 have a new-building or demolition permit within a block in the last two years, and 20 have major work permitted on their own lot in the last year. Each shows a note with what the permit says, and the tag "No building site next door" hides them. Limits: DC only for now; a permit is permission, not proof that work has started or is still going; and "major work" means the permit fee was $5,000 or more, which catches big renovations but also things like a boiler replacement. Hover the note to read the permit.
  • Balcony or terrace, and Own front door. Two new tags under "Unit", next to Ground floor. They are marked only when the unit's own listing says so, never from a building's "balconies in select homes". Today 566 units name a balcony, terrace or patio, and 137 say they have their own entrance or are townhomes; 126 of those are on the ground floor, in 14 buildings (tick Ground floor and Own front door together for your case). They come from the companies whose listings carry features per unit: UDR, Windsor, MAA, LyvWell and Gables, plus a handful at Greystar and Bozzuto. Most Bozzuto, Greystar and Camden listings say nothing per unit, so their units are simply unmarked; that is about two thirds of all units.
  • Top picks now adds things up in dollars. This is what I meant by tidying the deal tags; the three tags and their thresholds are unchanged. Before, Top picks ranked by which tag a unit had. Now each unit gets a sum in dollars a month: how far it is priced under similar units (counted up to 25%, since a bigger gap is more often a mismatch than a bargain), plus what you said a shorter drive, less violent crime, a more walkable block and quiet are worth. The list is ordered by that sum as a share of the unit's cost, so expensive homes do not win on size alone. Open any unit to see its sum under "Where it ranks in Top picks". Until you answer the Setup questions, only the price part counts.
  • Setup is now seven short steps, each with "Skip this step": the home, budget, where, commute, safety, getting around, quiet and the unit. "Finish now" keeps what you have answered; a skipped step changes nothing. Four steps ask a trade-off in dollars instead of "how important is it": for example "How much more rent per month would you pay to cut your drive to work by 10 minutes each way?" with $0 to $300, and a line under each saying exactly how the answer is used. The dollar options and the caps (no answer can count for more than twice its amount) are my calls.
  • Code violations: assessed, not built. Montgomery County publishes housing code cases by address. Matching two years of them to the 65 tracked Montgomery buildings: 37 have at least one real violation, the middle building has 4, the top quarter 15 or more, the worst 75. So it does separate buildings. Two cautions: the county inspects apartment buildings on a schedule, so one sweep of a large building can log 50 items that say more about its size than its upkeep; and the tracker does not know how many units a building has, to put the count per unit. The number of separate complaint cases, and serious items (infestation, no heat), look like the better signal. DC, where most tracked buildings are, has a look-up page but no open data I could find; Virginia publishes nothing. My view: worth building for Montgomery as a "complaint cases" line, modest value until DC is solved.
  • Needs you. Review text: the realistic source is Google's place reviews, fetched in your browser with your own Google key when you open a building (five reviews per building, a small cost per look-up). Say if you want that.

  • WC Smith is tracked, the first smaller local manager: 79 buildings, almost all in DC, 56 with something available today. These are the older buildings with bigger floor plans, such as Clarence House at Van Ness, The Cortland and Chatham Courts in Adams Morgan, and Battery Lane in Bethesda.   • Income-restricted buildings are now caught three ways. WC Smith also runs a lot of income-restricted housing and does not label it, and at first those topped the Local value list at 45% to 60% under their neighbours. Now:
    • In DC, a building that stands on a project in the city's public affordable-housing list, where at least half the units are income-restricted, is treated as income-restricted and hidden with the rest (7 buildings today). The list starts in 2015, so older ones are missed.
    • Any building on that list shows the city's count, for example "City records: 19 of 261 units here are income-restricted". That is 65 buildings, including many market-rate ones with a few set-aside units.
    • Anything 40% or more under similar units nearby is marked Check price, not Local value (58 units carry Check price today). This is my call, added to the earlier 30%-under-its-own-building rule.
  • Totals now: seven companies, 466 buildings found, 405 with prices, about 8,900 units. Tagged today: 126 Absolute steal, 62 Building steal, 504 Local value.
  • Tried and dropped: Polinger's site uses the same system as WC Smith but lists no buildings through it.

Published about 3:25 pm AvalonBay, first-visit setup, high-end label

  • "High-end for its area" replaces the bare percentage on each building.
