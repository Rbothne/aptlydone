What changed on the tracker, newest first, written while you were away. This page is temporary. Times are US Eastern, 18 and 19 November 2026.

Published about 12:20 am, 19 November seven more companies, tags drop-down, two more counties of crime, settings export

  • Seven more companies are tracked: Equity Residential (42 buildings), UDR (25), Cortland (20), Gables (13), LyvWell (10), MAA (5) and Simpson (4). Every one of those 119 buildings has prices today. Fourteen companies in all: 585 buildings, 528 with prices, about 11,500 units. Tagged today: 155 Absolute steal, 80 Building steal, 725 Local value, 96 Check price.
  • Tags are now a drop-down, like Areas. Three groups, each with an "(all)" box. Deals: Absolute steal, Building steal, Local value; a unit shows if it carries any one you ticked. Nearby: Near Metro, Grocery nearby, Park nearby, Dog-friendly. Building: Ground floor, Parking, Pets, High-end. In those two groups every box you tick must hold. This any-of / all-of split is my call. "Income-restricted & senior" stays a button of its own because it adds units instead of removing them.
  • Areas is a drop-down with several choices at once, grouped by state, with "Virginia (all)" and "Maryland (all)".
  • Crime for Howard and Loudoun, from the two dashboard links you sent. Each dashboard hands over a year of incidents with a map point and an offence code. Loudoun's covers the Sheriff's Office only, not Leesburg's own police. These are not a documented service, so they could stop working without notice; if so those counties keep their last counts. Alexandria and Prince William were added earlier tonight. Nine counties now, covering 559 of the 585 buildings.
  • Nearby places, walkability and a dog-friendly score. Each building lists what is close (Metro, supermarket, parks, dog park, community center, library, gym, pharmacy, places to eat), the EPA walkability score for its block (1 to 20), and a dog score out of 5. There is a Walkability slider and a "Most walkable" sort. Today 327 buildings have a Metro station within half a mile and 506 a supermarket; 291 sit in the EPA's top walkability band. The places come from OpenStreetMap's daily state files (the public OpenStreetMap query servers were all overloaded tonight and ask automated users to stay away), Metro stations from the transit authority's own list.
  • Parks are overstated for now. A park is measured to the rectangle around it, not to its real outline, so a building inside the rectangle of a long or large park (Rock Creek, the Parkway) reads "Park next door" when it may be several blocks away. 507 of 585 buildings currently count as having a park within a quarter mile, which is too many. Treat "Park nearby" and the park point in the dog score with caution until I measure to the real outline; that is the next fix.
  • Equity Residential could not be read from GitHub's servers. All 42 buildings read fine from this computer tonight, which is where today's prices come from, but the first automatic run on GitHub failed on every one. I cannot see GitHub's log from here, so failed reads now record their reason: a building whose latest read failed says, for example, "Latest read failed (HTTP 403)". If Equity turns out to refuse GitHub's servers, I will not work around that; its prices would then only refresh when the tracker is run from this computer.
  • Google Maps key in Setup, with the times you leave for and from work. With a key, drive times become rush-hour times for the next weekday, morning and evening. The key stays in your browser. I could not test this against Google because that needs a key; it is written to Google's documentation. Tell me what you see the first time.
  • Settings export and import in Setup: Copy or Save file gives you everything this browser remembers as text; paste it into another browser and press Load. The Google key is left out on purpose.
  • Laundry filter removed. The laundry note still shows on buildings that state it.
  • Adding a company is usually one line now. A company whose building pages describe themselves in the standard way and embed the SightMap unit map needs only a line in managers.json; Cortland, Gables, LyvWell, MAA and Simpson were added that way.
  • Looked at and left out. Dittmar, Mill Creek, Paradigm, Bernstein, ROSS, Daro, Van Metre and The Blairs answer automated visits with a challenge page, and I do not work around those. Kettler was not checked. JBG Smith, Keener, Polinger, Dweck and AIR are readable but each needs its own reader; they are next.
  • Known rough edges. MAA's Virginia buildings show the city as "DC Metro" (that is what MAA's pages say). UDR's building-wide specials are not read yet (none was on offer tonight, so I could not see their format). Equity's estimated utilities are not counted in monthly cost; its required monthly charges are.

Published about 3:50 pm WC Smith, and keeping income-restricted housing out of the deals

  • WC Smith is tracked, the first smaller local manager: 79 buildings, almost all in DC, 56 with something available today. These are the older buildings with bigger floor plans, such as Clarence House at Van Ness, The Cortland and Chatham Courts in Adams Morgan, and Battery Lane in Bethesda. Several now show as Local value.
  • WC Smith prices by floor plan, not by unit. Its site says "Studio A: 3 available, $1,550 to $1,655", not unit by unit. So each available floor plan is one line reading "3 available" and "from $1,550". There is no floor or move-in date for these.
  • Income-restricted buildings are now caught three ways. WC Smith also runs a lot of income-restricted housing and does not label it, and at first those topped the Local value list at 45% to 60% under their neighbours. Now:
    • In DC, a building that stands on a project in the city's public affordable-housing list, where at least half the units are income-restricted, is treated as income-restricted and hidden with the rest (7 buildings today). The list starts in 2015, so older ones are missed.
    • Any building on that list shows the city's count, for example "City records: 19 of 261 units here are income-restricted". That is 65 buildings, including many market-rate ones with a few set-aside units.
    • Anything 40% or more under similar units nearby is marked Check price, not Local value (58 units carry Check price today). This is my call, added to the earlier 30%-under-its-own-building rule.
  • Totals now: seven companies, 466 buildings found, 405 with prices, about 8,900 units. Tagged today: 126 Absolute steal, 62 Building steal, 504 Local value.
  • Tried and dropped: Polinger's site uses the same system as WC Smith but lists no buildings through it.

Published about 3:25 pm AvalonBay, first-visit setup, high-end label

  • AvalonBay is tracked (Avalon, AVA and eaves): 33 more buildings and about 700 units in the region (387 buildings and about 8,750 units in all), including Avalon at Foxhall, eaves Glover Park, AVA Ballston and Avalon Clarendon. Their pages give more than most: a rent, a total with required fees, the lease term, the rating, and promotions attached to the exact units they apply to. So for AvalonBay a "1.5 months off" promotion is counted in that unit's monthly cost, not shown as a maybe.
  • Setup on first visit. The first time the page opens it asks for bedrooms, smallest size, most you would pay, move-in date, work address, longest drive and household income, then shows results set up that way. Everything is optional and stays in your browser. The Setup button reopens it. You will see it once on your next visit because it is new.
  • Income flag. If you give an income, a unit whose rent is more than a third of it is marked "needs income of $X", the rule most managed buildings apply.
  • "High-end for its area" replaces the bare percentage on each building. A building is high-end when its one-bedroom cost per sqft is in the top third of buildings within two miles, mid-priced in the middle third, lower-priced below. There is a High-end filter too. This is my stand-in for "fancy". It holds on your three examples: The Earl, The Wendy and Windsor Courthouse are pricier than 89%, 78% and 96% of the buildings within two miles of them, so all three are labelled high-end.
  • Parking now says "Parking available, price not stated" when a building lists parking without a price, and the Parking filter accepts it.
  • Sturdier: if a manager's building pages start failing, the run stops asking that site after three failures and keeps yesterday's facts, while unit prices are still read. A neighborhood name corrected at discovery now shows up on the page.

Published about 2:55 pm follow a building

  • Follow a building, not just a unit. Each building has a "Follow building" button. It remembers the bedroom, size and price filters you had set at that moment, and the building then stays under Followed showing whatever fits them each day (or "Nothing here matches right now"). This is the "any one-bedroom of 700+ sqft under $X in this building" idea from earlier. The ☆ on a single unit still follows just that unit.

Published about 2:40 pm crime for more counties

  • Crime now covers five counties: DC, Montgomery, Prince George's, Fairfax County and Arlington. That is 273 of the 354 buildings.
  • Arlington's numbers are old, and say so. Arlington stopped publishing incidents with locations in June 2022; its newer "Crime Data Hub" shows totals only. I used the last year it published (June 2021 to June 2022) and each Arlington building says so on its crime line. It still separates one block from another. The Earl, The Wendy and Windsor Courthouse each had 32 to 34 violent incidents within half a mile that year.
  • Still no crime data: Alexandria (28 buildings), Howard (14), Loudoun (13), Anne Arundel (12), Prince William (7), Falls Church (5) and Manassas Park (2). I looked for an open list of incidents with locations for each and found none: Alexandria has a search form only, and the others publish dashboards or yearly totals. These buildings say "No crime data for this county".
  • Two cautions on the counts. County police data can miss incidents handled by a city's own police (Bowie and Herndon buildings show zero or near zero, which is probably that). And each county labels offences its own way, so compare across a border with care.
  • Census figures, including race and ethnicity, cover every building in all twelve counties (219 census tracts). No change was needed.

Published about 2:05 pm the big one

  • Three more companies. Greystar, Willow Bridge and Windsor are tracked alongside Bozzuto and Camden: 354 buildings found in the region, 293 with unit prices, about 8,000 units. The three buildings you named are in: The Earl, The Wendy and Windsor Courthouse.
  • Map tab. Every building that matches your filters is a dot, coloured by its best tag and sized by how many units match. Click a dot for its best unit and a jump to the list.
  • Drive times. Type a work address under the filters and press "Time my drive". Each building then shows a drive time, and there is a "Max drive" slider and a "Shortest drive to work" sort. These are no-traffic times, so they understate rush hour. The address stays in your browser.
  • Crime nearby. Buildings in DC and Montgomery County show reported violent and property crimes within half a mile over the past 12 months, with a "Violent crime nearby" slider. Other counties say "No crime data for this county" for now.
  • About the area. Each building has a fold-out with the census tract's income, rent, age, education, renter share and race and ethnicity, plus the ZIP code's typical rent and how it moved in a year. A switch at the bottom of the page hides race and ethnicity.
  • No-go ZIP codes. "No-go ZIP" on any building or map popup hides that ZIP code everywhere until you remove it from the line under the filters.
  • Rating and "Managed by" filters.
  • "Check price" tag. A unit 30% or more under similar units in its own building is no longer called a steal. It is almost always an income-restricted unit the building did not label, or a mistake, so it is marked "Check price" and sorted last. This is my call: before it, the top "Absolute steals" were units 40% under their neighbours with floor plans named things like "TEMP".
  • Faster and sturdier reads. SightMap and Bozzuto are read four times a second (a test showed no pushback). A site that stops answering is left alone after three failures in a row and come back to once at the end, at the slow speed.
  • Fixed: some managers put the street number where the ZIP code belongs; ZIP codes now come from a map lookup.
