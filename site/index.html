Monthly cost is the true monthly cost: rent spread over the lease after any free months stated for that unit, plus required monthly fees and required one-time fees spread over the lease. Parking and optional fees are not included.

Tags compare cost per square foot between units with the same number of bedrooms and a similar size. Building steal: 15% or more below similar units in the same building. Local value: 20% or more below similar units in other buildings within one to two miles. Absolute steal: 10% or more below its building and 15% or more below its neighbors. Check price: 30% or more below its own building, or 40% or more below its neighbors. That is usually income-restricted housing the building did not label, or a mistake; it is not counted as a steal. Price drop: the rent is at least 2% below the highest it stood at in the last 30 days and its latest move was down; a unit can carry it alongside a steal tag. Top picks puts first the homes with the most in their favour for their price: how far each is priced under similar units (counted up to 25%), plus the dollar amounts you gave in Setup for a shorter drive, less violent crime, a more walkable block and quiet, all taken as a share of the home's monthly cost. Open a unit to see the sum. In DC, a building standing on a project in the city's affordable-housing list where at least half the units are income-restricted is treated as income-restricted and hidden with the rest. Prices come from each building's own site and are only as good as what it publishes; check with the building before relying on one.

Crime counts are incidents reported to police within half a mile in the past 12 months, from each county's open data (so far: none). Counties count offences differently, so compare across a border with care, and county figures can miss incidents handled by a city's own police. Arlington's counts are from the last year it published locations, June 2021 to June 2022. A building's violent count is also given against the typical building: the middle one of all tracked buildings that have counts. About the area figures are for the census tract (American Community Survey, 5-year) and the ZIP code (Zillow's rent index). Who lives nearby limits the list by those tract figures: median age, the share of adults with a degree, and race and ethnicity. They only narrow the list; none of them feeds a deal tag or Top picks. The race and ethnicity limits go away, and stop applying, when the switch below is off.

Nearby places are from OpenStreetMap (Metro stations from the transit authority's list), measured in a straight line from the building; parks are measured to their edge. Walkability is the EPA's National Walkability Index for the block (1 to 20). Dog-friendly is out of 5: one point for allowing pets, two for a dog park within half a mile (one if within a mile), one for a park within a quarter mile, one for a dog run or pet spa on site; a building that does not say it allows pets gets no score.

Noise is from the US Department of Transportation's National Transportation Noise Map (2020): modeled sound from aircraft, roads and railways averaged over 24 hours, in bands. The Department says it is for seeing patterns, not exact enough to judge one address, so treat a note as "look into this". Aircraft noise is noted from 50 decibels and flagged from 55; road and rail from 60. Construction is from DC's building permits, so it is known for DC buildings only: a new-building or demolition permit within a block in the last two years, or alteration work on the building's own lot that paid $5,000 or more in permit fees in the last year. A permit is permission, not proof that work has started or is still going. Balcony or terrace and Own front door are marked only when a unit's own listing says so; most listings say nothing, and those units are simply unmarked.

Drive times are free-flow times with no traffic, so they compare buildings fairly but understate a rush-hour commute. Your work address is kept in this browser only. To time drives it is sent to the OpenStreetMap Nominatim service to find its coordinates, and those coordinates are sent to the public OSRM routing server along with the buildings' locations. On the map, opening a building draws its drive to work: a dashed straight line at once, then the usual road route without traffic, asked of the same OSRM server for that one building. The time shown beside it is the one from "Time my drive". If you paste a Google Maps key into Setup, drive times become rush-hour times for the next weekday, morning and evening: your work coordinates and the buildings' locations are then sent to Google with your key. The key stays in this browser and is left out of the settings export.
